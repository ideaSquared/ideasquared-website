How we work

One rule: prove it before we grow it.

Every idea gets a short proposal with the numbers that would mean “go”. If the numbers don’t come, it stops, and we write about why. It’s slower at the start and much faster at saying no.

Idea to product

Four stages. A decision at every gate.

  1. 01

    Idea

    Is this a real problem?

    We fill in a one-page idea card. A few hours, no spend. Most ideas stay here, and that’s fine.

    Gate: is it worth writing a proposal?

  2. 02

    Proposal

    What will we test, and which numbers mean go?

    One or two weeks of evenings, no spend. The proposal names who the product is for and how we’ll know it works.

    Gate: the go and stop numbers are written down.

  3. 03

    Project

    Does the smallest useful version work with real people?

    We build it and test it with a small group. Spend stays inside the studio cap.

    Gate: the results meet the go numbers.

  4. 04

    Product

    How does it grow and pay its way?

    Only now does a venture get its own business plan. We review it every year.

    Gate: a spin-out is a separate decision, taken when a venture outgrows the studio.

At every gate

Four possible outcomes.

Each decision goes in the decision log, with the reason.

  • GoThe numbers are met. Move to the next stage.
  • ReviseFix the main problem and retest. Once.
  • PausePark it until something changes.
  • StopEnd it, and write up what we learned.
A real example

The AdoptDontShop gate.

AdoptDontShop is at the Project stage. Its test is an 8-week closed beta with 3 to 5 rescues, planned for early 2027.

These are targets, not results. Completed adoptions are a stretch goal, not a requirement, because an adoption often takes longer than 8 weeks.

We’ll post the numbers during the beta, whatever they are.

The rules

Six rules keep a small studio honest.

Each one is a decision we wrote down. To change a rule, we change the decision first.

  • One focus at a time

    One venture gets the real hours. Right now that is AdoptDontShop. The others wait their turn.

  • Plan against real hours

    The studio runs on 6 to 16 hours a week, alongside a full-time job. Every date assumes that.

  • Test before we build big

    Every proposal sets its go and stop numbers before real time or money goes in.

  • Build in the open

    Public code where we can. Decisions written down, with the reason.

  • Evidence over hype

    We only claim what we can back with a dated source. A target is called a target.

  • Spend stays small

    Self-funded, with a cap of £200 a month. No investors and no office.
