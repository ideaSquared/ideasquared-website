What this is, and is not

AdoptDontShop is built and has no live users. Its closed beta is planned for early 2027. Rescues are often run by volunteers, and some of those volunteers use screen readers, keyboards or settings that reduce motion. We wanted the basics in place before anyone logged in.

This post describes work we did, using the dated entries on the venture page and in the build log. It is not a claim that the platform meets any accessibility standard.

First, checks on every change

On 9 September the platform started running automated accessibility tests on every update. Legal pages, such as the privacy policy and terms, were also linked from inside the app, so nobody has to leave to find them.

Automated checks catch only some problems. They do not replace using the app with a keyboard or a screen reader. But they stop the easy ones coming back, and the later work leaned on them.

Button colour, 11 October

The brand colour on primary action buttons failed the colour contrast standard. White text on the original rose tone measured below the required ratio. We darkened the colour. The journal says the aim was to make buttons more readable for everyone, including people with visual impairments.

A brand colour is a design choice. Here we changed it to meet the check.

Keyboards and tab panels, 1 November

On 1 November all form fields (text boxes, dropdowns, date pickers and text areas) gained a consistent focus indicator across the three apps. Tab panels in rescue settings, application review and the admin panel were also fixed so they work with keyboard navigation and assistive technology.

The late November run, 22 to 26 November

Most of the detailed work landed in five days.

  • 22 November. Date and checkbox filters got labels. Checkboxes and the top navigation bar are now announced correctly by screen readers.
  • 23 November. The Delete button on pet cards in the rescue app is easier to read when you hover over it or press it. Checkboxes show a clear outline when reached by keyboard, ticked or not. Chart header buttons work with screen readers.
  • 24 November. Sign-in, chat and the text editor show a clear outline on controls reached by keyboard. If your device is set to reduce motion, animations, transitions and smooth scrolling are switched off across the apps. Pages that scroll by themselves, such as pet search, the adoption application and chat, now jump instead.
  • 25 November. Screen readers now announce names that match the text on screen for the pet status filter, the admin dashboard cards, the user menus and the chat conversation list.

The 25 November fix matters more than it sounds. If a button says “Approve” on screen and a screen reader says something else, the two groups of users are using different apps.

What is still open

Nothing on the pages we drew from says AdoptDontShop has been audited or tested with rescue volunteers who use assistive technology, so we make no claim about either. The beta is where rescues will tell us. Our rule is to claim only what we can back with a dated source, so this post lists changes and stops short of a verdict.

What we learned

  • Automate first. Checks on every change stop easy faults returning, so human time goes on harder ones.
  • Names must match the screen. What a screen reader says should be what the page shows.
  • Respect the device settings. Reduce-motion and keyboard focus are things people have already told their device. We should listen.
  • Describe the work, not a verdict. Until real users and an audit say so, we only claim what we changed.
