The gate, as written

AdoptDontShop is at the Project stage: built, with no live users and no revenue. Its test is an 8-week closed beta with 3 to 5 rescues, planned for early 2027. On 8 November 2026 we wrote down the numbers that decide what happens next. They are on the home page and in how we work.

Go, if all four are met:

  • 3 or more rescues active, logging in every week;
  • 20 or more animals listed in total;
  • 10 or more adoption applications through the platform;
  • 2 or more rescues say they would keep using it.

Stop: fewer than 2 rescues active, or fewer than 5 applications, by week 8.

Anything in between: fix the main problem and extend by 4 weeks. Once.

Why completed adoptions are a stretch goal

You might expect the main number to be adoptions. It is not. Completed adoptions are a stretch goal, not a requirement, because an adoption often takes longer than 8 weeks.

So the gate asks for things that can happen inside 8 weeks. Do rescues come back each week? Do they list animals? Do adopters apply through the platform? Would the rescues keep using it? A rescue saying “yes, we would keep using this” is evidence we can use. A finished adoption is better evidence, and we will report any that happen, but we will not stake the decision on them.

Why publish first

The numbers are public on the home page, and the press page says the success criteria were published in advance. The home page puts the reason plainly: we wrote them down before the beta, so we can’t move them afterwards.

That matters to us for a specific reason. Our process says every proposal sets its go and stop numbers before real time or money goes in. We also have a venture, gaminginit, that we stopped without having written any. We covered that in We stopped gaminginit. A number you write after you see the result is easy to bend. A number that is already public is not.

The “extend once” rule

The middle case needs a rule too, because most real results land there. If the numbers are not clearly go and not clearly stop, we fix the main problem and extend by 4 weeks. Once.

The “once” is the important word. It matches the Revise outcome in our gates, which is to fix the main problem and retest once. Without it, a middling result can be extended forever, and an extension that never ends is a decision to do nothing. After the extra 4 weeks, the answer is go or stop.

What we don’t know yet

There are no beta results. The beta has not started. The platform has no live users yet, and the venture page says it is built and waiting for its first rescues.

The page also lists what we don’t know: whether rescues will use it unprompted, and whether adopters will apply through it. The beta numbers answer both. We will post the numbers during the beta, whatever they are.

What we learned

  • Pick numbers the test can reach. If the result you want can’t happen inside the test window, make it a stretch goal and gate on what can.
  • Publish before, not after. A public number can’t quietly move. That is the point of putting it on the home page.
  • Decide the middle case in advance. “Extend once” stops an unclear result from becoming an endless one.
