We stopped gaminginit: what we know and what we don’t.

Our process says every stop gets a write-up. This is the one for gaminginit, including the part where we never wrote down what would have made us stop.

  • By Alex Jenkinson, with support from Claude
  • 28 Nov 2026
  • 3 min read
  • Covers 30 Sep to 22 Nov 2026

What we stopped

gaminginit was a public website and a Discord bot for one game server community, the WARDOGS server. The site showed who was online, what map was running, match history and a leaderboard. The bot posted alerts and match results, and gave staff moderation commands. The two repos were gaminginit and wardogs-discord-bot.

The full platform launched on 30 September 2026. We shipped updates to it from 1 October to 7 November. On 21 November we stopped.

The reason we gave

This is the reason on the venture page, as written there: the game lost popularity, and it became difficult to keep a big enough player base, so we stopped working on it.

That is what we know. It is one sentence, and we are not going to stretch it into more. This post has no player counts, because we are not going to put a number here that we can’t source and show properly.

What the changelog shows, and what it doesn’t

The changelog on the venture page runs from 1–2 October to 7 November. It is a list of things we built: a live dashboard, a staff page, a coin system called INITcoins, support tickets, a Lifetime page for server history. The last feature entry, on 7 November, was a lighting control for staff.

That list tells you how much we built. It does not tell you whether it worked. A changelog counts output. It says nothing about whether the community grew or shrank, and we can’t read a result into it.

The gate we didn’t have

Our process says every idea gets a proposal with the numbers that would mean “go”, and that a stop ends with a write-up of what we learned. This post is that write-up.

The same process says go and stop numbers get written down before real time goes in. For gaminginit we have no such record. We did not write down what would mean go, and we did not write down what would mean stop. The written gate for AdoptDontShop, the closed beta gate, dates from 8 November 2026. gaminginit had launched on 30 September.

So the decision to stop was made without a number to compare against. We can say the reason we gave. We can’t say what level of players would have changed our minds, because we never said.

What the stop touched

Stopping a venture leaves work behind in the tools. In shipper, gaminginit was marked retired on 20 November. Deploys and rollbacks for it are refused, while status and logs still work so its history stays readable. The bot, gaminginit-bot, was marked retired on 22 November.

The same day, we removed both from agent-idea, the log tool. Its commit says both projects were deprecated in November 2026 and are no longer offered by its commands. One route word for the ideasquared-bot stays, because that bot still has the route.

Note the dates. shipper marked gaminginit retired on 20 November, a day before the 21 November stop date on the venture page. We have not found a written record that explains the gap, so we won’t guess at one.

What we learned

  • Write the numbers before launch. A stop with no stop number is a judgement call, and you can’t learn much from one. We now write the go and stop numbers first, as the AdoptDontShop gate shows.
  • A changelog is not evidence. A long list of shipped work can sit alongside a shrinking audience. Track the result you care about, not only what you built.
  • Write the stop up even when it is thin. The write-up is where we found out how little we had written down. That is worth publishing.
