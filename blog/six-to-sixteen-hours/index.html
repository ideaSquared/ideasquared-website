Running a studio on 6 to 16 hours a week.

ideaSquared is one person, part-time, alongside a full-time job. Here is how that shapes the rules we keep, and where it costs us.

  • By Alex Jenkinson, with support from Claude
  • 28 Nov 2026
  • 3 min read

The constraints, plainly

ideaSquared is run by Alex Jenkinson, in the evenings, alongside a full-time job. The studio runs on 6 to 16 hours a week. There is no staff. It is self-funded, with a cap of £200 a month, no investors and no office.

It has no revenue yet. Legal form: a sole trader, trading as ideaSquared. Incorporation is planned. These facts are on the about and press pages, dated November 2026, and we repeat them here so nobody has to look for them.

Plan against real hours

One of our six rules is to plan against real hours. Every date assumes 6 to 16 hours a week, not a good week. That is why the AdoptDontShop beta is planned for early 2027, and why we say “planned” and not “launching”.

The range is wide. A plan that only works at 16 hours is a plan that slips, so we plan for the low end and treat the high end as a bonus.

One focus at a time

Another rule is one focus at a time. One venture gets the real hours. Right now that is AdoptDontShop, and the other ventures wait their turn.

Pairflix shows what waiting means. It is at the Idea stage with a prototype and no public users. Its page says it has no proposal yet, that it needs go and stop numbers before it gets more than occasional evenings, and that this waits for the AdoptDontShop decision. We would rather say that than pretend two ventures are moving at once.

What every venture reuses

With few hours, you can’t solve the same problem twice. So each new venture picks up what the last one left behind. In our rules that is infrastructure (hosting, the GitHub organisation, the deploy pipeline and backups), process templates and the decision log, starting-point drafts for legal documents, and the brand.

The tooling part is on the tools page: shipper for deploys, Agent Idea for sanitised logs, the logger that refuses personal data, and eng for setting up a developer machine. A venture adds a ship.yaml, uses the logger and gets the rest from day one.

What it costs us

Pace is the main cost. A studio on evenings is slow, and it is slower at starting than a full-time team. Our process says as much: it is slower at the start and faster at saying no. Saying no matters when you can’t afford a wrong turn that takes months.

The money is the other cost, and we won’t dress it up. There is no revenue, and each venture sets its own revenue model only when it reaches the Product stage. Grants are applied for venture by venture. Until then, the cap is what limits us. We publish the £200 a month figure and nothing finer than that.

We also stopped a venture in November. gaminginit is the example. It shows that the “stop” outcome in our process is a real one.

What we learned

  • Plan for the low end. A range of 6 to 16 hours is honest. Dates built on 16 will slip, so we build on 6.
  • One focus beats many. We give the real hours to one venture and let the others wait. Waiting is a cost, and we show it on the page.
  • Reuse is the leverage. The hours we spent on shared tools are hours the next venture doesn’t need.
  • Say the money part out loud. No revenue, a sole trader, a cap. It is easier to trust a studio that says so.
